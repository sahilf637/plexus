# Plexus M3 — Topics

## Context

M0–M2 give a map of items joined by links. M3 gives it *places*: a tree of topics that is built
locally (no AI needed), named (locally, better with AI), shown on the map as regions that appear
as you zoom, and kept stable as the library grows. It also adds the optional slow "improve older
notes" pass, so notes saved before AI was switched on get the same titles, summaries, concepts
and links as new ones, and so topic names rest on good material.

Decided (2026-10-06):
- **Older notes**: improved slowly in the background, only when switched on in Settings, at most
  a set number per day (default 10, changeable), after new items, and never past a share of the
  daily limit that is kept for new items.
- **Topic look**: soft shaded regions with a label that fade in and out with zoom.
- **Editing**: rename only (a topic name you set is never replaced). No moving items between
  topics: there is no filing step.

## The tree

One variable-depth tree: top-level topics → sub-topics → … → items. Depth follows size, not a
fixed number of layers; "subject" is just the top level. The UI says "topic" at every level.

- **Built locally.** Membership is decided by graph clustering, never by the AI. The AI only
  names. This keeps topics stable and reproducible, and the feature works with no provider.
- **Fewer than 12 items**: no tree (the map is as it is today). Below that, topics only add noise.
- **Items that fit nowhere** (no links, no close neighbours) go to one top-level "Loose notes"
  topic, so nothing is hidden and nothing is forced into a wrong group.

### Building it (Rust, `topics/`)

1. **Graph.** Items are nodes. Edge weight combines: hand-made links (strongest), AI typed links,
   similarity links by tier (strong > related > maybe), and shared concepts (items tagged with the
   same concept pull together, which is how the mesh feeds the tree). Where an item has too few
   links to cluster by, its nearest neighbours by embedding are added at low weight, so the graph
   is connected enough to cluster.
2. **Multi-level clustering.** Louvain (modularity) at a low resolution gives the top level; each
   group is then re-clustered at a higher resolution until it is small (about 8 items or fewer),
   splits no further, or reaches depth 4. Nodes are processed in id order with a fixed seed, so the
   same library always gives the same tree.
3. **Tidying.** Groups under 3 items are merged into their closest sibling; a parent with one
   child collapses into it.
4. Runs on a background thread; target well under a second for a few thousand items. Nothing in
   the UI waits for it.

The frontend stops running its own Louvain for layout (`layout.ts`): it uses these topics, so
what is drawn and what is named never disagree.

## Staying stable

A map that rearranges when you paste something is a map you can't learn. So:

- **New items join, they don't reshuffle.** At T1, after linking, an item takes the topic most of
  its linked items are in (weighted by link strength), else the one whose centre it is closest to.
  Constant-time; the topic is marked changed.
- **Rebuilds are rare.** Done in the background when the library has grown by 25%, or 50 items
  since the last build, or after an older-notes batch, never more than once an hour, and only
  when idle.
- **Identity survives a rebuild.** New clusters are matched to old topics by how many members they
  share (at least half): a match keeps the topic's id, name and position. Only clusters with no
  match are new topics.
- **Names don't flap.** A topic is renamed by the AI only when its members have changed by more
  than 40% since it was named (checked by a hash of the member set).
- Topics are derived data tagged with `TOPIC_VERSION`; the whole tree rebuilds when the algorithm
  changes. Removing an item, or restoring one from the trash, updates counts the same incremental
  way; an emptied topic disappears.

## Names

- **Without AI (always)**: the most distinctive terms of a topic: its concepts when there are any,
  else terms common in it and rare elsewhere (TF-IDF over titles and text across topics). Two or
  three words. A sibling with the same name gets its next term added.
- **With AI**: one call per topic that needs it, giving a name (up to 4 words, in the language of the
  notes) and a one-line summary. Bounded context like everything else: up to 12 member titles with
  summaries (the most central ones), the names of its sub-topics (up to 12), its local terms and
  its siblings' names (to avoid clashes). Never the notes' full text. Cached by member hash and
  version.
- To keep cost small, AI names go to the top two levels; deeper topics keep their local names.
  Runs as its own lane (`Lane::Topics`) after new items and before older notes.
- **Rename by hand**: the name is then marked yours and never changes; "Reset name" goes back to
  the automatic one. Names are marked `auto | ai | you`, as titles are.
- Topics get a summary only when named by AI; the panel shows it if there is one.

## Improving older notes (deeper linking)

Settings → AI gets a group "Older notes":

- **Improve older notes** — switch, off by default.
- **Notes per day** — number, default 10 (0 means only the daily limit applies).
- A progress line: "124 of 380 done · about 26 days left at 10 a day", with the switch to pause.

How it works:

- **Which notes**: every note that was saved before AI was switched on, or never enriched at the
  current version, and isn't flagged as a secret. Most-linked first (they shape the topics), then
  newest.
- **Slowly**: a daily allowance is spread out (one note every few minutes while the app is open),
  rather than a burst, so it never competes with new notes. New items always go first. Counted per
  note, in its own counter beside the daily call counter.
- **Always keeps headroom**: older notes stop once calls used today reach 80% of the daily limit,
  so something you paste this afternoon is never refused because of a background job.
- **Same rules as new notes**: bounded context, content-hash cache, titles replace only
  auto-made ones, AI changes are marked and undoable. It adds typed links and concepts but never
  touches your hand-made links.
- A bad key or the daily limit pauses it exactly as they pause new notes.
- Because enriching an old note changes its links, the tree is re-checked after each batch.

This uses the existing `Lane::Backfill`; what is new is the pacing, the cap, the choice of notes,
and the setting.

## Map

- **Data**: the map payload gains the topics (id, parent, level, name, who named it, item count,
  centre and spread in map coordinates) and each item's topic. Ancestors come from the parent
  chain, so only the lowest topic is stored per item.
- **Semantic zoom**: three levels by zoom: zoomed out, top-level topics; middle, sub-topics;
  zoomed in, item labels as now. Labels cross-fade between levels. Dots are always drawn.
- **Regions**: a soft, faint shaded area behind each topic of the current level (a blurred disc
  sized by how widely its items spread, tinted by a muted colour per top-level topic), drawn on a
  canvas under the items, redrawn when the camera moves (coalesced to a frame). Labels sit on the
  topic's centre in a fixed screen size, and avoid overlapping: the biggest topic wins.
- **Click a topic label**: the view zooms to fit it; everything outside is dimmed (the same
  mechanism as search); a panel opens (below). Esc clears. The magnetic hover and cursor-drift
  keep working.
- **Layout follows topics**: each leaf topic gets a patch, children are arranged inside their
  parent's region, ForceAtlas2 refines. Positions are saved as now, so a rebuild keeps matched
  topics where they were; only new topics get placed. "Tidy" lays the whole thing out again.
- **Topic panel** (docked, like the item panel): name (click to rename), summary if any, a path
  ("Rust › Ownership") whose parts are clickable, sub-topics with counts, and the items most
  central first.
- **Item panel**: the same path above the title.
- **Topic list**: a button in the header opens a collapsible tree of topics with counts, for
  jumping around and for people who prefer a list to a map.
- **Settings**: nothing extra needed for topics themselves; "Regroup topics" sits under Library for
  the rare case a rebuild is wanted now.

## Data

Migration 7 (derived tables; rebuilt from the notes like everything else):

```
topics(id, parent_id, level, label, label_by, summary, named_members, centroid, version)
topic_members(item_id PRIMARY KEY, topic_id)      -- lowest topic only
```

`named_members` is the topic's items (a JSON list) when the AI named it, to tell later how much it
has changed; `centroid` is the mean vector of its items, for placing new ones. The map works out
each topic's centre and spread from the items' live positions, so they aren't stored.

`meta` keys: `topics_built_at`, `topics_built_items`, `topics_version`, `topics_changes` (things
that changed what topics rest on since the last build), `ai_older_<date>` (older notes improved
today). `label_by = you` is the one thing here that isn't derivable; see the open decision below.

## Backend pieces

- `topics/graph.rs`: build the weighted graph (links, concepts, nearest neighbours).
- `topics/cluster.rs`: multi-resolution Louvain, merge tiny groups, collapse single children.
- `topics/matching.rs`: carry ids, names and positions across a rebuild.
- `topics/assign.rs`: the incremental join for new and restored items.
- `topics/names.rs`: local terms; the AI naming request and applying its answer.
- `enrich/`: `Lane::Topics`; the older-notes scheduler (pacing, daily note count, 80% reserve,
  most-linked-first order).
- `settings.ai`: `improve_old: bool`, `improve_per_day: u32` (default 10).
- Commands: topics, rename/reset a topic, regroup, older-notes progress. Events: `topics-changed`,
  and progress through `enrich-status`.
- Mock backend: groups its sample items into topics so `npm run dev:ui` shows all of it.

## Tests and measuring

- Clustering is deterministic: the same library gives the same tree.
- On the seed library (about 230 notes in 15 topics): the tree should recover most of the 15 groups
  with high purity. A `tune_topics` report (ignored test, like `tune_links`) prints purity, the
  number of topics per level and a few examples, to set the resolutions.
- Stability: adding 5 notes to the seed library moves at most a few percent of items between topics;
  matched topics keep their ids and names.
- Matching, incremental join, merge of tiny groups, collapse of single children.
- Naming against a fake provider (as `llm::http_tests`): bounded request, caching, a hand-set name
  is never replaced, AI names stay on the top two levels.
- Older notes: respects the per-day number, the 80% reserve and the secret filter; new notes jump
  ahead of it; pauses on a bad key.

## Status

All of it is built, and the backend is covered by tests (clustering, graph, matching, local and
AI names, the older-notes scheduler, rebuild timing). Differences from the plan:

- **Tuning** (`tune_topics` on the seed library of 218 notes in 15 topics): top level at
  resolution 0.5 and sub-groups at 1.2 give about 10 top-level topics and 40 lowest ones, the
  lowest 96% pure, and no seeded topic is split across top-level topics. At 0.5 related topics
  merge into areas (geology, climate and astronomy under one parent), which is the hierarchy
  wanted; higher resolutions give one top-level topic per subject instead.
- **Names** are better than planned for the local fallback: concepts first, then distinctive
  terms shown as written (not stemmed), a topic that is a mix of sub-topics is called after them,
  and a sub-topic avoids the words its parents use.
- **Rebuilds** are counted over linked items (a freshly imported library is built once its notes
  are linked, after things go quiet for a few seconds, not from the first dozen). A small tree
  (under 100 items) may be rebuilt every two minutes as it grows; a big one at most hourly.
- **Layout** starts from the topics when there are some, and from the old Louvain grouping in
  `layout.ts` otherwise (fewer than 12 items).
- The AI naming request is covered by tests of what is sent and how answers are cleaned; the
  provider call itself shares `enrich::call` with item enrichment and was not run against a live
  provider in development.

`npm run export-map` now exports topics too, and
`PLEXUS_SEED=<folder> cargo test index_seed -- --ignored` builds a seeded library's index (with
topics) so the browser-only UI can show them.

## Open decisions

- **Where hand-set topic names live.** They are the only data here that a rebuild from the notes
  folder can't recreate. Recommendation: keep them in the database for now (renames are rare) and
  say so; if it matters, write them to a small file in the library later, keyed by a few member
  ids so they re-attach.
- **Seed-library tuning** may change the resolutions and the 8-item and depth-4 limits above.

Not here, by design: the Ask agent (M4), imports, scale work (M5).
